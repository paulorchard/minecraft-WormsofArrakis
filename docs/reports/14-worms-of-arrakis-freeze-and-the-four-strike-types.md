# Report — Prompt 14 Worms of Arrakis: the freeze and the four strike types
Status: partly — all five parts are built, the engine-free rules are covered by 280 passing unit tests, and a dedicated-server smoke start ran the whole chain (stalk, freeze, scan, choice, placement, devour moment, wait for the move, end) on a fake player; but no real player and no client was involved, so the kill of a real player, how the freeze feels on a client, and how the placed worm looks round the victim are unseen.   Commit / tag: `strike-1` = b7957ae (Part 1); `strike-2` and `strike-3` are both on the one later commit (Parts 2 to 5 share the strike runner, so they could not be split; `git log` has the hash)

## What was built
**Part 1, the freeze (`strike-1`).** `Freeze` (engine-free: a linear cut 0 to 1 over AttackSeconds, the jump block from the first tick, a hold at zero, an idempotent release) with a `Body` interface; the adapter's `FreezeRun` is the body: two transient attribute modifiers (movement speed `ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL` of minus the cut, jump strength of minus 1), dismount, no elytra, and a push-back past 0.6 blocks from the spot. `FreezeHook.onFreezeStart/onFreezeEnd` (defaults only log) for the camera prompt. `HuntEvent` phases are now STALKING, FREEZE (the old ATTACK), STRIKE, ENDED.

**Part 2, alignment and the devour (`strike-2`).** `Alignment` (pure): the preset spec of `/arrakis-worm <size> <move>` played once (loop off), the entry crossing (where the head tip first meets the sand surface, refined by bisection from the move's own window), the origin of the entity so that the entry crossing is at the victim, the devour time derived from the move's frames. `WormSpawner` (test package) puts the entity in the level with the same calls as the command. Config `DevourSeconds.<move>.<size>` (16 keys) and the damage type `islandcraft_wormsofarrakis:swallowed` (data file, bypass tags, death message in the language file). `DevourGate` decides once.

**Part 3, availability and odds (`strike-2`).** `Corridor` (crest corridor and engulf disc), `StrikeChoice` (availability, rescaled weights, the choice, the log line), `ScanTerrain` (a per-tick column budget so the check never blocks the tick), config tables `CrestAhead/CrestBehind/CrestTolerance`, `StrikeWeights`, `StrikeYawMode`, `ScanColumnsPerTick`. `hunt trigger ... strike <type> [force]`.

**Part 4, the strike (`strike-3`).** `StrikeRunner` (adapter): scans during the last quarter of the freeze, chooses at the end of it, spawns the worm, kills at the devour moment, waits for the worm to remove itself, cleans up on any end. `HuntEvent` STRIKE phase (`finishStrike`, `setStrikeLimit`, `markDevoured`), status shows the phase, type and direction.

**Part 5 (`strike-3`).** `Prompt14FreezeTest` (11 tests) and `Prompt14StrikeTest` (25 tests); a test-only `hunt fake <x> <y> <z>` and `hunt fake trigger <size> [strike <type> [force]]` that run an event on a NeoForge fake player (the smoke server has no real player).

## Inferred    (not verified)
- **The kill of a real player.** The fake player refuses damage (the log says `could not be killed (damage and death were both refused)` and the freeze is released), so `hurtServer` of the damage type followed by `die` was not seen to kill anyone. The totem bypass is proven only by the tags being loaded and by reading the vanilla code; absorption by `bypasses_effects`; not by a real death. The owner's first real strike is the first test of the kill, the hidden body, the death message and the drops.
- The freeze on a real client: that the attribute modifiers hold without rubber-banding and how smooth the slowdown is (see answer 1).
- **Entry crossing.** I took "the point where the head first meets the sand surface" as the first instant the head tip is above the base level (the move's own `window()` start, refined). The strike-alignment previs could not be read; if it measured the crossing as the head ring, not the tip, every move is off by about a head-length cone (a few blocks at W8, up to about 10 at W48). The owner's look will show it.
- **The sand level.** The move's base level is the top face of the sand block under the victim (block y + 1, the height of their feet). `/arrakis-worm` in its `here` mode uses feet minus 1; I did not copy that.
- **The devour times** (answer 3): crest values are a derivation plus a guess (head length over speed); crash, emerge and engulf are "the head tip at its highest", also a guess for "the mouth is round the player".
- The client's animation clock starts at the entity's spawn, the server counts the worm's `tickCount`; network delay can shift the kill against the picture by the latency.
- A forced type is checked at the command (the victim's place then) and not again at the strike: if the victim has moved to a place where it is unavailable, it still runs (logged).

## Answers to the prompt's questions
1. **How the freeze holds the player.** Two transient attribute modifiers on the victim, updated every tick: movement speed `ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL` of minus the cut (the speed they had, times 1 - cut; the cut is linear 0 to 1 over AttackSeconds = 4 s) and jump strength `ADD_MULTIPLIED_TOTAL` of minus 1 (from the first tick). Also each tick: dismount (`stopRiding`) and no elytra (`stopFallFlying`); once the cut is 1 the spot is anchored and a victim pushed or pearled more than 0.6 blocks away is put back with a server teleport (below that nothing is done). The attribute change is synced to the client, which scales its own input by it, so client and server agree and no position is forced during the slowdown; I expect it to be smooth but it was not seen. **Leaks (inferred):** swimming up in water (the vertical impulse of a jump in a fluid does not use the jump attribute), ice and slime (momentum from before the freeze runs out slowly, a hold only when it carries more than 0.6 blocks), knockback from a mob under 0.6 blocks, and a pearl is undone only after the freeze reaches zero. Looking, items and chat are untouched. **After a crash or a log-off:** the modifiers are transient (not saved with the player), so they vanish with the player object; the log-in handler also removes them by their id as a belt and braces. A respawned player is a new object with no modifiers; the freeze is released at the devour at once.
2. **Placement.** The preset of `/arrakis-worm <size> <move>` (the same spec builder), as one run (loop off). The entity's model +Z is the way the worm travels; the origin is the victim position shifted back by the model z of the entry crossing along that direction, which is the shift `TestCommand.place` makes with `centreZ` (I use the entry crossing in its place). Entry crossing z (model, blocks) crest 33.22 / 52.63 / 91.58 / 130.69; crash 19.40 / 28.80 / 47.60 / 66.40; emerge 18.00 / 26.00 / 42.00 / 58.00; engulf 19.59 / 28.80 / 47.60 / 66.40 for small / medium / large / huge. No adjustment of any move or size was needed or made (the owner's previs said none is). Yaw: crest the passing direction (S 0, W 90, N 180, E 270); the others a random cardinal direction (config StrikeYawMode, `random` allowed). Vertical: the top face of the sand block under the victim.
3. **Devour times** (seconds after the move starts; config `DevourSeconds.<move>.<size>`, logged at start with the derived values next to them): crest 2.92 / 4.58 / 7.91 / 11.26; crash 4.14 / 7.45 / 14.08 / 20.70; emerge 3.79 / 5.84 / 9.93 / 14.02; engulf 3.17 / 5.01 / 8.94 / 12.86 (small / medium / large / huge). Derivation: the crest is the tip crossing (2.50 / 3.83 / 6.50 / 9.19 s) plus the head length over the speed (head 5 blocks at W8 over 12 blocks per second, scaled with W); the other three are the instant of the highest head tip between the crossing and the end of the advance (emerge: the end of the rise; engulf: the start of the hold; crash: the end of the run, 1.7 to 10 s after the crossing). **Guesses:** all of them in the sense that "the mouth encloses the player" is not a number in the moves; the crash is the least certain (its head is still rising at the end of its run, so the mouth may be round the player earlier). Note for the owner: the victim stays held from the start of the freeze until the devour, so a huge crash holds them 4 + 20.7 s.
4. **Hidden and killed.** At the devour moment `setInvisible(true)` and then `hurtServer(level, source, Float.MAX_VALUE)` with the damage type `islandcraft_wormsofarrakis:swallowed`; if the player is still alive after that `die(source)` is called; if even that fails the invisibility is undone and a warning is logged. The message is the language key `death.attack.islandcraft_wormsofarrakis.swallowed` = `%1$s was swallowed by a sandworm`. **A totem does not save them:** the damage type is added (never replacing the vanilla list) to the tags `bypasses_invulnerability` (vanilla's `checkTotemDeathProtection` returns false at once for it), `bypasses_effects` (absorption), `bypasses_armor`, `bypasses_resistance`, `bypasses_enchantments`, `bypasses_shield`, `bypasses_cooldown`; the start-up log line proves the tags loaded. Drops fall where they died; hardcore is an ordinary death. Not verified on a real player (see Inferred).
5. **Corridor and disc reads.** Worst case (every direction passes, so nothing stops early) per attack, in columns: small 209, medium 377, large 725, huge 1,073 (192 / 360 / 708 / 1,056 for the four directions plus the 17 of the disc); per direction 48 / 90 / 177 / 264. A column is one height-map read and one block read. Early failure stops a direction at its first bad sample, and columns shared by directions are read once (the smoke run on flat sand read 141 distinct columns at W8). **Off the tick:** the scan starts at three quarters of the freeze (the victim has about 0.7 blocks of travel left, at a sprint), reads at most ScanColumnsPerTick (60) new columns a tick (the 1,073 of a huge worm take 18 ticks of the 20 left), by re-running the check on a cache that throws when the tick's budget is used up, so no column is read twice and nothing waits. At the strike the result is used if the victim is within 1 block of the scan's spot (and on the same ground); otherwise the columns are read again at once (at most about a thousand reads, well under a millisecond; logged). **Unloaded chunks** are a failed sample (flagged, one log line per attack); no chunk is loaded or forced.
6. **The end callback of the move runner.** There is no callback; there are two things: the entity removes itself (`TestWormEntity.tick` discards a loop-off move at `MoveFactory.finished`, i.e. play time + `REMOVE_MARGIN` 0.5 s) and `Moves.Move.playTime()` gives the run time. The event waits for the worm entity to be removed (checked every tick) and then calls `finishStrike()`; as a safety net `setStrikeLimit(playTime + 0.5 + 3 s)` ends the event if the removal never reports; an early end discards the worm. Run times (s): crest 11.73 / 21.57 / 41.22 / 60.86; crash 7.73 / 11.84 / 19.62 / 27.13; emerge 9.59 / 13.68 / 21.86 / 30.05; engulf 5.14 / 7.38 / 11.91 / 16.33.
7. **Tests changed.** One line of `Prompt13Test`: `HuntEvent.Phase.ATTACK` is now `FREEZE` (the phase was renamed). `Prompt13TextTest` now expects one more key in the language file (the death message). Everything else of prompts 01 to 13 is unchanged and passes (the older tests that ran an event to its end still do, because without a strike runner the STRIKE finishes at once).
8. **What blocks the camera prompt and the later mobs and totems.** Nothing blocks. The camera prompt plugs in at `FreezeHook` (`HuntAdapter.setFreezeHook`), `TremorFx.camera` (prompt 13) and the `onStrike`/`onPhaseChange` hooks; the strike worm entity is in `StrikeRunner` (one per event). Mobs and totems: the aggro registry (`WormAggro`) is unchanged; a mob as a victim would need the freeze `Body` to be implemented for a mob (the interface is engine-free) and `StrikeRunner.devour` to take a living entity. A second victim standing within a worm width of a strike is only logged.

## Surprises, problems, deviations from the prompt
- **The kill was not verified** (fake players refuse damage), nor the freeze on a client; see Inferred. I added a start-up check of the damage type and its tags to have at least that proof.
- **A dedicated server pauses when nobody is online** (`pause-when-empty-seconds`, default 60): my first smoke run did nothing because the hunt never ticked. I set it to -1 in the dev server's `server.properties` for the smoke run and restored the file. (The same would have hidden the prompt 13 smoke result; that one only ran commands.)
- **The long hold.** The worm's script starts with a tunnel lead, so the crossing is 2.5 to 11 s after the move starts and the devour 2.9 to 20.7 s; the victim is held (frozen) all that time on top of the 4 s freeze. That follows the prompt ("devour time in seconds after the move starts") but is a long time to stand still for a large or huge worm; the owner may want to start the worm earlier (a lead time) or shorten the tunnel in a later prompt. I did not change the moves.
- **Parts 2 and 3 and Parts 4 and 5 share one commit** (tags `strike-2` and `strike-3` are on the same commit) because the strike runner uses both; `strike-1` is its own commit. The jar was deployed after Part 1 and again at the end, not after each of the later parts.
- The default of ScanColumnsPerTick is 60 (I started at 40, which would not have covered a worst-case huge scan in the 20 ticks).
- A test-only `hunt fake` command exists (operator only, documented in the log). It is how the server smoke could run a whole event; it should be removed or kept as the owner prefers.
- The scan is evaluated again every tick on its cache (a few hundred cheap cache hits a tick) rather than being a resumable state machine; simple and bounded, not minimal.
- A forced type (not a forced direction) is all the command offers; the direction of a forced crest is chosen as usual among the passing ones (any, if forced on rock).
